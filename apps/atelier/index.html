HOW THIS WAS BUILT the garment saga: five generations from armour to fabric-flow · where the motion comes from · what the instruments prove ▾
  1. ①

    Fitted rings · v4 · geno-outfit.js

    Garments authored as fitted ring meshes wrapped around each body part, sized by hand in bind pose. Two failure modes, no middle: off the skin it gaped (holes at the armpits), on the skin the body swallowed it — and padding it out to stop the swallowing read as American-football armour. (The founder's verdict, verbatim, is quoted in geno-cloth.js.)

  2. ②

    Full cloth-sim · geno-cloth.js — retired

    The textbook answer: real PBD cloth at 1/240 s substeps, capsule colliders rebuilt from the body every substep, waistbands pinned to bones. It draped beautifully — and jittered at stride extremes, and burned CPU while the avatar stood still. The sizing war was lost differently: collision replaced sizing but brought its own chaos. Kept in the repo, retired from this UI.

  3. ③

    SKIN-DERIVED · the turn · v5 · geno-derived.js

    The founder's insight: “you know the dimensions of the body.” A garment is the body's own triangles — whole triangles, so the topology is inherited and cannot tear — pushed +6 mm along their own normals, wearing the body's own skin weights. It cannot be inside the flesh (it is the flesh, offset) and cannot billow into armour. Zero solver: skinning is the only per-frame cost.

  4. ④

    Contour hems + graded hang · v6 → v7

    Body-derived alone left jagged frontier hems — the “apocalypse survivor” cut. v6 cuts every opening on a smooth plane and zips the boundary to a regular 64-sample contour ring; a finished-edge band (+2.2 mm, vertex-tinted) makes it read as a hem. v7 grades the hang — collar +6 mm → chest +12 → hem +18 mm — with baked pleats and sag: loose, never armour, zero runtime cost.

  5. ⑤

    Fabric-flow · v8 → v9 · today's default

    v8 rebuilt shirt + shorts as ring-lattice fabric with their own topology — clothing patterns hung straight from the chest, regularised sections smoothing the anatomy (no body print). v9 adds “easy physics”: spring-dampers on the free hem rings only (~770 verts), world-space, capsule push-out so hems never tunnel. Fabric sways with velocity, settles in under a second, costs nothing at rest — and the FROG rides the Head bone through all of it.

The instruments — what each one proves

👁 X-ray
Garments at 30% opacity (+ optional body wireframe). Proves seams track the body through the fabric — the human-eye check the founder asked for.
🌡 Seam heatmap
Every garment vertex false-coloured by live distance to the body surface: green <2 cm (hug) · amber <5 cm (drift) · red >5 cm (float) · white-cored red = inside the body (swallowed). Turns the graded-hang millimetres into something you can see.
▶ Attachment probe
The numbers, not the eyes: poses every clip × 4 phases + every exercise × 3 phases, then measures garment→body distance, inside-body count (bar: 0), Δsource (garment verts must ride their body verts), silhouette bulk ≤ body +1.5 cm (the armour detector) and hem regularity (the torn-end detector).

Mocap — where the motion comes from

  • Geno is the demo biped of Meta's ai4animationpy — the character from the founder's Unity game jam; 23-joint mixamo-family skeleton.
  • The five goblin_*.bvh files were a mislabelled demo capture — no goblins; they ship honestly as “stick walk / limp / drag (old)”.
  • 16 real clips (100STYLE subjects, CC BY 4.0) retargeted world-quaternion → joint name in BVHPlayer.
  • Soldier walk/run/idle preferred for neutral locomotion; aim walk / floor scoot / get down are flavour.
  • Licence split (docs/20 §6): motions CC BY — fine. The Geno mesh is research-only → the commercial-safe swap lives on the /avatars Meshy cards.
